## A. Premium streetwear / collegiate / youth brands

### 1. Aimé Leon Dore — collegiate codes, updated
- URL: https://www.endclothing.com/ie/features/aime-leon-dores-latest-drop-brings-collegiate-style-codes-bang-up-to-date [S] (the same END feature exists under /be/, /jp/ and /kr/ paths)
- Also seen: https://www.endclothing.com/be/features/aime-leon-dore-deliver-retro-sportswear-and-graphic-colours-for-ss19 [S]
- Category: Premium streetwear / Ivy-collegiate
- What it shows: Queens-born label (2014) mixing Ivy staples, normcore sweats and workwear. END's coverage describes SS19 as hip-hop plus collegiate in colour-blocked pieces, and a later drop built on "stately emblems and jewel-tone colourways".
- Design lesson: Collegiate does not have to mean loud. Emblem-led graphics, a few saturated "jewel" colours against neutrals, and classic silhouettes read as grown-up. Restraint carries the look.
- How we apply it: Put one small emblem (our own Surus-in-compass mark) on the chest of most pieces. Let Tyrian purple act as the "jewel" colour on cream grounds, and keep lime to accents only. Use no school-crest imitation and none of the label's own marks.

### 2. Rowing Blazers — heritage crests, pop-culture wit
- URL: https://www.ivy-style.com/you-dont-know-jack-an-interview-with-rowing-blazers-founder-jack-carlson.html [S]
- Also seen: https://hypebeast.com/2026/2/j-press-jack-carlson-ss26-runway-show-collection-release-info-interview [S]
- Category: Collegiate / heritage revival
- What it shows: Founder Jack Carlson trained in archaeology and classics, worked on crests and coats of arms at the College of Arms, and wrote a book on rowing-club blazers. The brand reinterprets British and American preppy dress (rugby shirts, striped blazers, collegiate knitwear) with a street-informed, pop-aware eye. (Search summaries conflict on the founding year: one says 2017.)
- Design lesson: A founder with real historical literacy makes heritage feel specific, not costume. Stripes, crests and colour-blocking work because they follow real club/college rules.
- How we apply it: Our archaeology/classics link is literally the project (Punic Carthage). Build a small "heraldry" system: a shield or roundel with Surus, Λ-peaks and the cohort year, plus a stripe code (purple/cream/lime) for rib trim and scarf ends.

### 3. Awake NY — script wordmark, saturated colour, place-based graphics
- URL: https://endclothing.com/ie/brands/awake-ny [S]
- Also seen: https://remezcla.com/?p=483505 (Gap collaboration) [S]; https://www.dezeen.com/2023/06/17/awake-ny-store-lower-east-side-rafael-de-cardenas/amp/ (store design) [S]
- Category: Streetwear / New York identity
- What it shows: Angelo Baque's label uses bold New York references, early-90s East Coast hip-hop colour-blocking, Shirt Kings-style graphics, a script wordmark and print-heavy jerseys. The Gap collaboration kept its identity by re-lettering "Gap" in an Awake font. (Launch date conflicts: 2012 vs 2018 debut at NYFW.)
- Design lesson: A distinctive, repeatable wordmark treatment lets collaborations and different garments all feel like one brand. Rooting the graphics in a specific place gives them authority.
- How we apply it: Make one custom "HANNIBAL / هنبعل" lettering treatment (A replaced by Λ) and reuse it on every garment, which is brand coherence at low cost. Anchor the whole range to Tébourba/Medjerda rather than generic "Tunisia".

### 4. Online Ceramics — type-heavy graphic tees, dye as a design variable
- URL: https://juicestore.com/blogs/editorial/online-ceramics-ss20 [S]
- Also seen: https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/grateful-dead-tie-dye-print-trend/ [S]
- Category: Graphic-tee / subculture merch
- What it shows: Los Angeles label (2016, Alix Ross and Elijah Funk) built on Grateful Dead imagery: hand-drawn graphics, hand tie-dyed tees and sweats, text-heavy lore imagery. Each hand-dyed piece varies. Sold in limited drops.
- Design lesson: Dense, lore-rich text and illustration is acceptable when the subject has real mythology. Dye variation makes each piece feel unique.
- How we apply it: The Hannibal/Surus story is our "lore". Print a back graphic with the Alps route and annotated labels. Consider a garment-dyed or over-dyed purple tee batch where slight variation is a feature.

### 5. Cav Empt (Sk8thing) — graphic-led, cerebral typography
- URL: https://www.collater.al/en/sk8thing-shinichiro-nakamura-style/ [S]
- Also seen: https://sabukaru.online/articles/sk8thing-in-the-shadows [S]; https://hbx.com/journal/2026/09/cav-empt-questioning-the-familiar [S]
- Category: Japanese streetwear / graphic design
- What it shows: Founded in 2011 by Sk8thing and Toby Feltwell. Graphic-heavy designs combine unexpected imagery on familiar silhouettes. The name comes from the Latin "caveat emptor", via Philip K. Dick's novel *Ubik*. AW18 explored glitch and pixel themes. Sk8thing also did early A Bathing Ape graphics.
- Design lesson: Plain, familiar silhouettes plus unusual graphics and cryptic text make a garment feel designed, not decorated. A Latin motto used as a brand device fits this tradition.
- How we apply it: Keep tees, hoodies and caps boxy and ordinary. Put the intelligence in the graphic: a Surus drawn with compass arcs, with small cryptic-but-correct annotation text.

### 6. Stüssy — a signature as a logo
- URL: https://www.collater.al/en/stussy-logo-history-style/ [S]
- Also seen: https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/stussy-50-million-brand [S]
- Category: Heritage streetwear / wordmark
- What it shows: The logo began as Shawn Stussy's marker signature on surfboards around 1980, then moved to tees, shorts and caps. White screen-print on black tees (1982) helped sales at a trade show. The brand name was later stylised "Stüssy". (Dates differ between sources, so treat them as approximate.)
- Design lesson: A hand-made mark, printed in one colour on a plain garment, can become the whole brand. One-colour logo printing is cheap and reads confidently.
- How we apply it: Develop a hand-drawn, compass-and-ruler "signature" mark for the cohort. Make a one-colour (cream on purple, or purple on cream) chest print the baseline tee, with no extra graphics.

### 7. Carhartt WIP — reinterpreting workwear, keeping the cut language
- URL: https://us.carhartt-wip.com/pages/about [S]
- Also seen: https://hbx.com/life/brands/carhartt-work-in-progress [S]
- Category: Workwear-to-street
- What it shows: Carhartt (Detroit, 1889) is the parent. Swiss founders Edwin and Salomée Faeh started Carhartt Work in Progress in Europe, with a licence to make their own lines in 1994 (some listings say 1989). WIP slimmed and modified original cuts, and the brown duck coat became a streetwear icon. The Detroit jacket is among its best sellers. (Search results did not confirm the Detroit jacket's design date.)
- Design lesson: Workwear works in fashion when you keep its honest construction (canvas, patch pockets, plain labels) and change proportion and colour, not the logic.
- How we apply it: For the chore-coat/workwear jacket, use heavy cotton duck or drill, patch pockets and a small woven label. Cut it slightly cropped and boxy for teens. Dye it deep purple or natural cream, not camouflage or "military".

### 8. Noah — a classic rebuilt with better materials
- URL: https://www.gearpatrol.com/style/noah-fw26-campus-jacket/ [S]
- Also seen: https://www.mrporter.com/en-kw/journal/fashion/introducing-noah-your-new-favourite-brand-1152742 [S]
- Category: Premium streetwear / coach jacket
- What it shows: Noah (Brendon Babenzien and Estelle Bailey-Babenzien) redesigned the coach's jacket as the AW26 "Campus Jacket": pure wool melton shell instead of nylon, plaid twill lining, medium-wale corduroy collar lining, a snap throat latch, while keeping the elastic cuffs, drawcord waist and snaps. The brand stresses ethical mills and manufacturing.

- How we apply it: Offer the coach jacket in a plain, matte shell (cotton-nylon or poly twill, per budget) with a contrasting lining (printed Λ-pattern or cream). Put a small embroidered mark on the left chest and a large back print. Add a woven label at the neck.

### 9. Kith × Russell Athletic × CUNY — small-college pride, done premium
- URL: https://queenseagle.com/all/kithhitsqueenscollege [S]
- Also seen: https://eu.kith.com/blogs/kith/kith-fall-2022-lookbook [S]

### 10. Champion Reverse Weave — construction as heritage
- URL: https://thehundreds.com/blog/a-brief-history-of-champion-enduring-reverse-weave/ [S]
- Also seen: https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/features/2019/09/champion-athleticwear-celebrates-100-years/ [S]; https://www.championstore.com/pages/about [S]
- Category: Heritage sportswear / sweatshirt construction
- What it shows: Champion says it developed Reverse Weave in 1934 and applied for a patent in 1938 (granted 1952, per one source). The fabric is cut on the cross grain so it shrinks widthwise, not lengthwise. Side gussets and stretch-knit side panels came with later models, not the first one. (The search found nothing on "ribbed inserts" beyond the side panels.)
- Design lesson: Heavyweight, boxy, cross-grain-feeling fleece with ribbed side panels is what a premium "campus hoodie" signals. It is the construction, not the logo, that justifies the price.
- How we apply it: Specify a heavyweight (e.g., 400 gsm+) brushed-back fleece for the hoodie, with ribbed side inserts and a ribbed hem/cuff. Do not claim "Reverse Weave" (a Champion trademark).

## C. Varsity, coach and workwear construction

### 15. Varsity jacket anatomy
- URL: https://therealmccoys.com/products/wool-varsity-jacket-bulldogs_030-black [S]
- Also seen: https://shop.stfx.ca/Catalogue/shop/clothing/jackets/melton-leather-lettermans-jacket [S]; https://www.boozt.com/eu/en/superdry/college-patched-varsity-jkt_33050401 [S]
- Category: Garment construction
- What it shows: The search found vendor product pages, not a construction manual. Together they show: a melton wool body (one maker describes oil-reduced, milled melton; a heavy 24 oz spec appears), leather sleeves (cowhide or horsehide; faux leather on cheaper versions), rib-knit collar, cuffs and hem (cadet rib, nylon/wool blends, often striped), snap front, slanted pockets (often leather-edged), quilted or satin lining, and chenille patches plus embroidery on chest and sleeves.
- Design lesson: The jacket's identity is the body/sleeve contrast, the striped rib trim and the snaps. Everything else can be simplified for a student budget.
- How we apply it: Body: wool-blend melton in dark purple. Sleeves: cream PU (the lycée needs a low-cost, leather-free option). Rib: purple/cream/lime stripe. Snaps: matte or tonal. Lining: quilted polyester. Patch placement: chenille on chest, embroidery on one sleeve. (Spec numbers are vendor-specific; ask the factory for its own.)

### 16. Chenille patch construction and artwork limits
- URL: https://www.worldemblem.com/patches/chenille/ [S]
- Also seen: https://patents.google.com/patent/US10941509 [S]; https://assets.stahls.com/stahls/content/pdf/flyers/STAHLS-Chenille-Patches-Specs-Sheet.pdf [S]; https://assets.transferexpress.com/pdf/specs/TXP-Chenille-SpecSheet.pdf [S]
- Category: Technical / patch rules
- What it shows: Patches are built from one or more felt layers with chenille yarn stitched over them. Edges outside the stitching can curl (per a patent). Backings can be heat-applied, peel-and-stick, sew-on or hook-and-loop. Sew-on is the usual choice for wool and leather/vinyl. Per the vendor summaries: smallest chenille character about 30 mm high with at least 3 mm stroke; text of 30–45 mm in block fonts only; internal cut-outs about 10 mm; maximum 6 chenille thread colours; direct embroidery on the same patch at least 6 mm high with 1 mm columns; felt border about 4 mm; minimum patch size 1" × 1" at most vendors (2" × 2" at Stahls).
- Design lesson: Chenille is a bold-and-simple medium. Fine lines will fail.
- How we apply it: Draw the chenille patch as 1–2 letterforms or a simple Λ shape on a felt shield. Put all small text in embroidery or print instead. Keep the stroke at 3 mm minimum on the final patch size.

### 17. Coach jacket construction
- URL: https://universalworks.com/blogs/articles/the-coach-jacket [S]
- Also seen: https://www.gearpatrol.com/style/noah-fw26-campus-jacket/ [S] (see #8); https://ironheart.co.uk/products/ihj-160-blk [S]
- Category: Garment construction
- What it shows: Typical build is a nylon twill shell (one product cites 4.8 oz; some are resin-treated), a snap placket, pointed collar, drawcord waist, elastic cuffs, two welted hand pockets and an interior stash pocket, with a polyester taffeta or cotton lining. Universal Works places its roots in 1960s–70s US stadium/athletic-department jackets, often in team colours with "COACH" across the back. (The windbreaker lineage and the first maker are not established in these sources.)
- Design lesson: The big back graphic is part of the garment's DNA, so it is the right place for the hero artwork.
- How we apply it: Hero Surus-compass print on the upper back (DTF or screen), small embroidered mark on the left chest, and no extra pocket graphics.

### 18. French chore coat / workwear history
- URL: https://www.taylorstitch.com/blogs/archive/the-hardworking-history-of-the-french-chore-coat [S]
- Also seen: https://analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/chore-coat-history [S]; https://us.pand.co/en-ca/blogs/news/a-brief-history-of-the-french-chore-jacket [S]
- Category: Workwear history and construction
- What it shows: Roots in late-19th-century French bleu de travail, usually cotton drill or moleskin in deep blue, boxy and collared, with three or four patch pockets and button closure. French makers (Vetra, Le Laboureur, Mont Saint Michel) began serial production in the 1920s. American versions include Levi's (roughly 1880 "Sack Coat") and Carhartt's Engineer Sack Coat (1923 or 1925; sources disagree). Dye claims (indigo vs a benzoate-based blue) conflict between sources.
- Design lesson: The French origin gives the lycée's bilingual French/Arabic audience a natural cultural link. Plain, practical and honest construction reads premium.
- How we apply it: Use a deep purple or ecru chore coat with four patch pockets and corozo-look buttons. Add a woven label and a discreet tone-on-tone embroidered Λ.

## D. Embroidery, print, label and scarf technical rules

### 19. Embroidery file rules
- URL: https://www.customink.com/help_center/embroidery-design-tips [S]
- Also seen: https://members.asicentral.com/news/how-to/may-2018/keep-your-small-letters-crisp/ [S]; https://www.gelato.com/au/blog/creating-the-perfect-embroidery-file [S]
- Category: Technical / embroidery
- What it shows: Custom Ink recommends text at least 0.25 in (about 6 mm) high and satin lines at least 0.05 in (about 3 pt / 1.3 mm) thick. Thinner lines become single run stitches or are removed. The ASI article argues that column width matters at least as much as height: a satin column cannot be narrower than the needle, so finer lettering needs finer thread (e.g., 60-weight) and needle (70/10) together, and small letters should use centre-walk, not edge-walk, underlay. Gradients, negative-space objects and distressed art may not embroider well.
- Design lesson: Design for the stitch from the start. Thin geometric hairlines (our compass arcs) must be drawn at embroidery width or replaced.
- How we apply it: Make a separate "embroidery master" of the logo with arcs at 1.3 mm or thicker, at most 3–4 thread colours, and text at least 6 mm tall. Avoid fine Arabic hairlines; embroider Arabic only at heavier weights and larger size, and ask the digitizer for a stitched proof before bulk.

### 20. Screen vs DTF vs DTG, and print artwork preparation
- URL: https://www.gelato.com/connect/article/dtg-vs-screen-printing-vs-dtf [S]
- Also seen: https://printify.com/blog/dtf-vs-screen-printing/ [S]; https://stitchi.co/blog/screen-printing-artwork-preparation-6-guidelines [S]; https://www.printablepress.com/how-to-prepare-artwork-files-screen-printing/ [S]
- Category: Technical / print method
- What it shows: Screen printing is the most durable and cheapest per unit at volume. Sources cite about 100 units and up and 1–4 spot colours (some say gradients up to roughly 9 colours). DTF suits small runs (about 25–500 per Gelato) and works on cotton, polyester, blends and dark fabrics. DTG needs no setup or minimum, is best on natural fibres and is weaker on polyester. Minimum-order and wash-count figures conflict between vendors, and Gelato says its numbers are indicative. For screen print, artwork should be vector, in spot colours (Pantone labelled), with trapping and registration marks. Halftone frequency is about mesh ÷ 5 (e.g., 45 lpi on a 225 mesh). One source suggests 0.5 pt lines as a safe minimum and 8 pt for text. RGB should not be used.
- Design lesson: Pick the print method before drawing the art. The colour count and line weight follow from it.
- How we apply it: Tees and back prints: 2–3 spot colours (purple, cream, lime) for screen printing at 100+ units, or DTF for small size-run batches. Keep lines at 0.5 pt or heavier and text at 8 pt or larger on the final print. Provide Pantone references and let the printer test a swatch.

### 21. Woven label specification
- URL: https://superlabelstore.com/blog/woven-label-design-and-best-practices/ [S]
- Also seen: https://superlabelstore.com/woven-labels [S]; https://www.griffesvivienne.com/en/woven-labels/materials/ [S]
- Category: Technical / branding trim
- What it shows: One supplier says text weaves cleanly down to about 2 mm in width and advises larger sizing if there are more than two lines of text. Yarn is 50, 75 or 100 denier. Damask has the highest thread density, is the softest and most common for garments, and costs more. Taffeta is cheaper and has a lower density. Sizes quoted are finished sizes after folding. Labels can be centre-fold, end-fold, Manhattan or mitre. Text that is too small for the label is the most common reason a proof comes back. I found no published standard for minimum text height, thread count per inch or fold allowance.
- Design lesson: A woven label is a tiny, expensive graphic. It needs the simplest version of the logo.
- How we apply it: Create a label master with the wordmark only (Latin and Arabic stacked), at least 2 mm stroke, using a damask weave on the neck. Ask the factory for a physical proof.

### 22. Jacquard-knit scarf
- URL: https://sense2.com/product/200436 [S]
- Also seen: https://www.igift.hk/Scarf055-en.html [S]
- Category: Technical / scarf
- What it shows: Custom knitted jacquard scarves are offered with up to four PMS colours knitted into the fabric (matching is approximate), a size such as 1500 × 180 mm, and machine-knit fringe, hand-tied fringe or none. Minimum orders cited: 500 for machine-knit fringe, 100 for hand-tied or plain, and MOQ applies per artwork design. Sample time is 10–14 days; production time is 21–28 days after sample approval (another vendor says 30 working days). No gauge was given in any result.
- Design lesson: In jacquard knitting, the pattern is built into the yarn, so graphics must be coarse and blocky.
- How we apply it: Draw the scarf as a stripe-and-Λ-peak pattern in 3–4 colours (purple, cream, lime, plus a dark). Do not attempt compass hairlines. Plan 6–8 weeks of lead time and ask the factory for knit gauge and a strike-off.

### 23. Chèche (Tuareg-style) cotton scarf
- URL: https://horniman.ac.uk/object/HC.2017.45 [S]
- Also seen: https://www.perceptivetravel.com/issues/0113/tuareg.html [S]; https://decathlon.ae/products/desert900-desert-trekking-tagelmust-blue [S]
- Category: Technical / cultural textile
- What it shows: A museum entry describes an indigo-dyed tagelmust/cheche as an unhemmed length of cloth worn by the Tuareg as a turban against desert extremes. Retail examples are 100% cotton, around 95 g, about 2.6 m long (one seller gives 80 cm × 2 m). I found nothing in these results on the chèche's history in Tunisia specifically.
- Design lesson: A lightweight cotton wrap is an easy, low-cost scarf format with a real regional echo, but the Tuareg tagelmust is a specific cultural garment.
- How we apply it: If we make a woven or printed cotton scarf, call it a "foulard" or "écharpe", not a "chèche", and use our own Λ-stripe design. Do not copy Tuareg or Berber patterns.

## E. Typography, wordmarks and annotation graphic language

### 24. Type specimen posters as a layout model
- URL: https://www.berlin-international.de/en/snapshots/projects/type-specimen-posters/ [S]
- Also seen: https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/comd1127-type-media-ol39-f20/2020/08/05/project-3/ [S]; https://madegooddesigns.com/poster-design-principles/ [S]
- Category: Typography / graphic language
- What it shows: Type specimen posters show what a typeface can do. Teaching sources emphasise a grid (for example a baseline grid in 5 or 10 mm steps and a 12/16/24-column grid), hierarchy and contrast, and one assignment limited the palette to black plus one spot colour with no imagery. **Honest note:** my searches found no source documenting scientific diagrams, annotation labels or specimen sheets as a trend in streetwear graphics (Brain Dead, Gramicci, Pop Trading all returned illustrative, not diagrammatic, examples). Treating "scientific annotation" as a trend would be unsupported.
- Design lesson: The annotation language can still work as a graphic system, because specimen sheets use a grid, numbered callouts and small captions to make a large drawing look authoritative.
- How we apply it: Number every part of the Surus drawing (1 Fig. Surus, 2 Défense / عاج, 3 Compas) in a small mono face on a baseline grid. Keep callouts to 2–3 per graphic. Label things that are true (e.g., "Tébourba 36.83 N 9.83 E") so the label is content, not decoration. Present this as our own concept.

### 25. Bilingual Arabic–Latin typography
- URL: https://www.khtt.net/en/page/587/fresco-arabic-western-latin-meets-western-kufi [S]

## G. Cultural and historical facts for accuracy

### 30. Hannibal Barca and the ancient accounts
- URL: https://www.worldhistory.org/timeline/hannibal/ [S]
- Also seen: https://www.britannica.com/print/article/483518 (Second Punic War) [S]; https://m.johndclare.net/AncientHistory/Hannibal_Sources3.html (Polybius and Livy side by side) [S]; https://www.livius.org/articles/person/hannibal-3-barca/hannibal-in-the-alps [S]
- Category: History
- What it shows: Hannibal lived about 247–183 BCE (the birth year is approximate). He was the son of Hamilcar Barca and took command in Spain after Hasdrubal the Fair was assassinated in 221 BCE. The Second Punic War began in 218 BCE (end date given as 201 or 202 BCE). He crossed the Alps in 218 BCE, won at Cannae on 2 August 216 BCE, was defeated by Scipio Africanus at Zama in 202 BCE, then went into exile and died by suicide to avoid capture. Polybius (3.42–3.56) and Livy (21.28–21.38) give almost identical accounts of the Rhône and Alps crossings, probably from the same eyewitness source (possibly Sosylus of Lacedaemon). Scholars agree the elephants crossed the Rhône by raft.
- Design lesson: Use the real chronology. The Alps (218 BCE), Cannae (216 BCE), Zama (202 BCE) are enough for a timeline label.
- How we apply it: Annotation text and the back-print timeline should use only these dates. Mark approximations with "c." The lycée's name honours Hannibal, so a respectful tone is better than a cartoon.

### 31. Surus the elephant — what the ancient sources actually say
- URL: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_romana/gladiators/elephantus.html [S]
- Also seen: https://www.britannica.com/animal/Surus [S]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surus [S]
- Category: History / accuracy
- What it shows: Pliny the Elder (Natural History 8.5.11), preserving a fragment from Cato, says one elephant survived the campaign: Surus ("the Syrian"), ridden by Hannibal and regarded as the bravest, with a **broken tusk**. Wikipedia says the name may possibly mean "One-Tusker". Scholars (Lazenby, Scullard) conclude the evidence for Surus being Asian rather than African is "intriguing but ultimately ambiguous". The coin evidence shows North African elephants. Livy places Hannibal on the surviving elephant in the Arno marshes, where he lost the sight of one eye. The common figure of 37 elephants at the Alps crossing appears in a modern teaching text, but I did not see the ancient passage stating it.
- Design lesson: Do not say "one-tusked Surus" as fact. The ancient detail is a broken tusk; "one-tusker" is a modern etymology proposal.
- How we apply it: Draw Surus with one tusk broken or shortened (this fits the source) and label it "Surus — « le Syrien »" with a footnote-style "défense brisée (Pline, H.N. VIII, 11)". Avoid claiming it was an Indian elephant.

### 32. "Aut viam inveniam aut faciam" — is it genuinely Hannibal's?
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inveniam_viam [S]
- Also seen: https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/aut-viam-inveniam-aut-faciam.228766/ [S]; https://groups.google.com/g/alt.language.latin/c/tvoHctLb4_Q [S]; https://latindiscussion.org/threads/ill-either-find-a-way-or-make-one.10237/ [S]
- Category: Quotation sourcing
- What it shows: The search summaries agree there is **no ancient source** (Livy, Polybius) for Hannibal saying this. The phrase traces to Seneca's *Hercules Furens* (cited as around line 276, with some posts citing 275 ff.), where Amphitryon says in the third person "inveniet viam aut faciet". The first-person "I shall either find a way or make one" is a later adaptation, popularly attributed to Hannibal. One wiki-mirror source suggests the attribution may be a Renaissance-era embellishment, but I could not verify that dating. The phrase was used as a motto by Francis Bacon and Robert Peary.
- Design lesson: A glossy motto with a false attribution is a credibility risk on a school collection.
- How we apply it: Either (a) print it as a motto without any name ("Aut viam inveniam aut faciam"), or (b) credit "adapté de Sénèque, Hercule furieux". Do not print "— Hannibal".

### 33. Tyrian purple — dye chemistry, colour range and the Tunisian link
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrian_purple [S]
- Also seen: https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Tyrian_purple [S]; https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Murex_purple [S]; https://archaeology.org/news/2020/01/15/200116-djerba-meninx-survey [S]; https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/74153 [S]
- Category: Colour / history
- What it shows: Tyrian purple is a reddish-purple natural dye whose colouring compound is 6,6'-dibromoindigo, from predatory sea snails of the family Muricidae (murex). The secretion is yellowish and oxidises in sunlight to purplish red. Hexaplex trunculus can give a blue result (linked to tekhelet), so the family spans red-purple to blue-violet depending on species and processing. About 12,000 Murex brandaris yield no more than 1.4 g of pure dye. A synthetic equivalent was made in 1904. The Phoenicians (from Tyre) founded Carthage, and the dye was a main source of Phoenician, Carthaginian and Roman wealth per Tunisia's heritage institute. For Tunisia, excavations at Meninx on Djerba (Tunisian National Heritage Institute and LMU Munich) found crushed Hexaplex trunculus shells everywhere on site and show purple-dye production as a central pillar of its economy from the Punic to the Late Roman period. Pliny named it the source of Africa's best purple. Little evidence survives on Carthage's own workshops, and no documents detail the methods.
- Design lesson: Authentic "Tyrian purple" is not one hex value. It is a crimson-leaning purple to violet range, so the collection's purple can be a deep magenta-leaning plum rather than a blue-purple.
- How we apply it: Choose a plum/crimson-purple (and say "inspired by", not "Tyrian purple, exact") and confirm the Pantone/thread match with a physical swatch. The brandkit.pro hex values I saw conflicted and are not authoritative. I did not confirm any Pantone code for Tyrian purple. Mention Meninx, Djerba as the Tunisian dye-making site in the annotation text.

## Key takeaways for this collection

1. **Restraint reads premium.** ALD, Todd Snyder and the 2025 leavers-hoodie trend all use one small emblem or embroidered crest, not an all-over print. Make the small Surus-compass mark the front hero on nearly every piece.
2. **Back-of-garment storytelling.** Coach jackets and hoodies carry the large graphic or name list on the back (coach-jacket tradition; "tour-poster" leavers names). Put the Alps route and the student names there.
3. **One repeatable wordmark** (Awake, Stüssy) keeps all pieces looking like one brand. Build a single HANNIBAL / هنبعل lockup with Λ for A and use it everywhere, including on the woven label.
4. **Plain silhouettes, clever graphics** (Cav Empt, Carhartt WIP). Keep cuts boxy and ordinary; spend the creative effort on the artwork.
5. **Construction sells the price.** A varsity jacket needs melton-look body, contrast sleeves, striped rib and snaps; a hoodie needs heavy fleece with ribbed side panels; the coach jacket needs a drawcord hem and snap placket.
6. **Draw for the medium.** Embroidery lines at 1.3 mm or thicker with text at 6 mm or taller, chenille at 30 mm characters with 3 mm strokes, jacquard scarves in blocky 3–4 colour patterns, screen print at 0.5 pt lines or heavier. Make separate masters for each.
7. **Colour discipline.** Purple/cream/lime works as neutrals plus one jewel plus one accent; keep lime under about 10% of any garment. Tyrian purple is a red-leaning purple to violet range, so choose and verify with a swatch.
8. **Accuracy is a feature.** Surus had a broken tusk (Pliny citing Cato); the "aut viam inveniam aut faciam" line is Seneca-derived, not Hannibal's; Tébourba is Thuburbo Minus (not Majus). Printing correct annotations makes the "specimen" idea credible.
9. **Bilingual by pairing structure.** Match x-height, stroke contrast and weight between Latin and Kufi. Reem Kufi, Noto Kufi Arabic and Cairo are OFL and contain Arabic. Test at real size, and keep Arabic at heavier weights for embroidery.
10. **Budget path.** Inkscape, Krita and Blender are free (GPL); Affinity is free in its 2026 form; Figma has a free tier. Skip CLO 3D / Marvelous Designer subscriptions. Plan 6–8 weeks for scarves and ask every factory for proofs.
