## 3. General cost-structure data (all `[NON-TN estimate]`)

These are foreign market figures (mainly US/EU, USD or EUR) from vendor blogs and price sheets. They give the *logic* of pricing, not Tunisian prices. Tunisian labour and consumables costs differ, and the search tool itself noted that these sources are not always consistent.

### 3.1 Screen printing
- Setup is charged **per screen, one screen per colour**. Reported range USD 15 to 50 per screen per colour; a 4-colour design is then USD 60 to 140 in screen fees before any shirt is printed. Sources: InkSoft help (https://help.inksoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/46724059783579-Screen-Print-Pricing), PrintablePress (https://www.printablepress.com/screen-printing-cost-breakdown/), uniforms.com.au (https://uniforms.com.au/blogs/uniform-insights/screen-printing-prices-for-uniforms-workwear-and-merchandise-at-com-au).
- Per-piece add-on per extra colour reported as roughly USD 0.75 to 1.50 depending on run size.
- Artwork prep USD 25 to 75 per hour if the art is not print-ready vector. Reorders on the same screens often get a reduced or waived setup fee.
- Breakeven versus digital methods commonly cited at about 24 pieces. Labasni (Tunisian, `[TN-listed]`) says about 20 pieces.

### 3.2 Embroidery
- Pricing logic = **stitch count x rate per 1,000 stitches + digitising (one-time per design) + garment**.
- Benchmark rate USD 1 to 3 per 1,000 stitches; one shop example USD 3 per 1,000, 3.50 above 3 colour changes, 4 at 10 or more colours (one shop only).
- Digitising USD 20 to 60 per design (Fash.com), or USD 25 to 75 one-time (shop intake form). Many shops fold it into the unit price.
- Small chest logo of 1,000 to 5,000 stitches costs about USD 6 to 15 for embroidery alone (excluding garment). A French maker states a minimum of 10 pieces, back area limited to 20x20 cm, heart/chest to 8x8 cm.
- Sources: Printful (https://www.printful.com/blog/how-to-price-embroidered-products), Fash.com (https://Fash.com/costs/embroidery-cost), zdigitizing.com (https://zdigitizing.com/how-much-does-embroidery-cost/).
- Appliqué/chenille (varsity letter) pricing logic was **not found**. Ask for it per piece.

### 3.3 DTF
- Priced by **film area** (per linear metre/inch of a gang sheet, or per A4/A3 sheet), not by colour count.
- US examples: USD 0.45 per linear inch on a 22-inch-wide sheet standard (about USD 17.70 per metre, our conversion), wholesale tiers to about USD 0.32 per inch; USD 0.04 to 0.055 per sq inch at Transfer Express. Sources: https://dtftransfersnow.com/products/dtf-transfers-wholesale-html , https://www.transferexpress.com/videos/dtf-gang-sheets-for-ultracolor-max , https://dtfdallas.com/blogs/news/dtf-cost-per-transfer-math-breakdown-print-shop .
- French example (EUR): A4 transfer 3.00 EUR HT per unit, no minimum, degressive by metre: https://tissus-print.com/en/dtf-printing/tp-101157-dtf-print-a4-format.html
- Then add the heat-press labour. No Tunisian DTF per-metre or per-A3 tariff was found: not published, request quote.

### 3.4 Marking price benchmarks, France (EUR per piece, `[NON-TN estimate]`)
- Companeo guide: screen print about 1 to 5 EUR, flocage/DTF 3 to 15 EUR, DTG 5 to 20 EUR per piece (https://www.companeo.com/objets-publicitaires/guide/tee-shirt-publicitaire).

### 3.5 MOQ patterns
- `[TN-listed]` Shops (Labasni, Teewinek, Cadeau Tunisie) advertise 0 to 25 piece minimums depending on technique: screen print around 10 to 20, embroidery on quote from 25 (Teewinek).
- `[TN-listed]` Export-oriented contract factories (TFH page): 500 to 5,000+ pieces. JC2S MOQ not published.
- `[NON-TN]` Woven labels: some European label makers start at about 500 pieces (Etiquettes-textile.com). Metal/enamel pins from China: B2B listing minimum 2,000 pieces (Global Sources). For 60 to 200 students, plan on buying a bigger bundle or using a local pin seller.
- Sector context (press): about 80 percent of Tunisian textile companies are fully export-oriented. A fully-exporting company may not be allowed or willing to sell on the local market, and a 60 to 200 piece run is far below typical export MOQs. **Confirm** with any factory whether they accept a local order.

## 4. Tunisian context

### 4.1 Using the school's name and logo
- **Unknown.** No Tunisian circular or law was found on student-group clothing bearing a lycée's name or crest.
- Indications only (not legal sources): the Ministry of Education logo is described on Wikimedia as a registered trademark, so it should not be used freely on merchandise; Tunisian uniform suppliers (IB PRO) offer garments "customisable with the school logo", showing the practice exists; in France a common dress is written into the règlement intérieur (analogy only).
- Practical path (suggested, to be confirmed): written request to the lycée director with the artwork, items and quantities; for the ministry's logo or the national emblem, do not use it without written approval; ask the regional education office (commissariat régional) if the director is unsure. Check INNORPI for any registered mark if a logo is registered.

### 4.2 Import and regulation
- For a school-group order the simplest route is **local production in Tunisia**. Only generic items (enamel pins, woven labels) may tempt overseas buying.
- Tunisian customs tariff rates for apparel (HS chapters 61, 62) and fabric: **not found**. The only figures found related to second-hand clothing (2023 finance law) and are not applicable. Customs duty, VAT, shipping, and any import formalities on sample or bulk parcels must be obtained from the Direction Générale des Douanes or a freight forwarder.

## 5. Cost estimation framework (TND)

Per-item landed cost per student, for a run of N pieces of item i:

```
C_i = B_i + M_i + L_i + T_i + (S_i + D_i + P_i) / N + W_i
```

- `B_i` = blank/garment (or fabric + trims if cut-make-trim): either unit blank price, or `fabric_m_per_piece x fabric_price_per_m x (1 + waste%) + trims`
- `M_i` = marking cost per piece, by technique (below)
- `L_i` = labour for CMT only: sewing/making charge per piece (ask the "façon" price)
- `T_i` = trims per piece: zips, rib, drawcords, snaps, interlining, woven label, hang tag, polybag
- `S_i` = one-off setup for item i: pattern/grading, sample (prototype), screens, cliché
- `D_i` = digitising (embroidery) and artwork prep
- `P_i` = pre-production sample(s), shipping of samples
- `W_i` = wastage/spoilage allowance (typically add a % for misprints, size swaps)
- N = total pieces of that item (not the cohort size)

**Marking cost per piece (choose technique):**
- Screen print: `M = sum over placements of (colours_p x per_colour_per_piece_rate) + ink/underbase surcharge`, plus one-off `S = screens x screen_fee` (screens = number of colours x placements).
- Embroidery: `M = (stitches / 1000) x rate_per_1000_stitches x (1 + colour_change_surcharge)` per placement; `D = digitising fee per design`.
- DTF: `M = film_area_per_piece (cm2) x price_per_cm2 (or m_film x price_per_m / pieces_per_m) + press_labour`.
- Appliqué/chenille letter (varsity): `M = piece_cost + attachment labour`. Ask supplier for per-letter or per-patch price.
- Patch/label: `unit_price(MOQ tier) + sewing/attachment labour`.

**Group totals:**
```
Budget_total = sum_i ( N_i x C_i )  +  design/prototype fees  +  logistics (collection or delivery to Tébourba)  +  VAT (if prices are HT)  +  contingency (suggest 10-15%)
Price_per_student = Budget_total / students  (or bundle price per pack)
```

**Break-even test between techniques:** screen print beats DTF/flex when `S_screens + N x M_screen < N x M_DTF`, i.e. `N* = S_screens / (M_DTF - M_screen)`. Labasni states about 20 pieces as the crossover, `[TN-listed]` (to be confirmed with real quotes).

**Variables to request from each supplier (all in TND, state HT or TTC):**
1. Unit price at tiers (for example 50, 100, 150, 200 pieces), per item.
2. Blank spec: fabric composition, g/m2, brand/quality, colour availability, size range.
3. Cost per screen/colour and number of screens; per-colour per-piece rate; ink type.
4. Embroidery: price per 1,000 stitches or flat price per placement, max stitch count/area, digitising fee, thread colours.
5. DTF: price per A3/A4 or per metre, max print size per garment.
6. Sample/prototype cost and time; cost of size set.
7. Trim costs; labels (woven vs printed), hang tags, packaging.
8. Lead time from approved sample to delivery; payment terms; deposit %.
9. Delivery cost to Tébourba or collection point.

## 6. Questions to send in every RFQ

1. Do you accept a local order of 60 to 200 pieces, and what is your minimum per item and per design?
2. Please quote each item at 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 units, in TND, stating HT or TTC.
3. What exactly is the blank (fabric, g/m2, composition, shrinkage, colour fastness)? Can you send a swatch or photo of a similar finished piece?
4. For screen print: price per screen, per colour per piece, max colours, ink type (water-based or plastisol), wash durability.
5. For embroidery: price per 1,000 stitches or per placement, digitising fee, max size, thread type, backing.
6. For DTF: price per A3/A4 or per metre, max print size, wash-test results.
7. Can you do appliqué/chenille letters and PU or leather sleeves for a varsity jacket? Who sources the melton?
8. Which items can you make in-house and which are subcontracted? To whom?
9. Do you offer pre-production samples? Cost, lead time, and can they be deducted from the order?
10. Sizing: do you have a size chart, can you do a size run for fitting, and what is the policy for re-sizing or misprints?
11. Lead time from approved sample to delivery, and the latest order date for delivery by the target date.
12. Payment terms, deposit, invoice (facture) with tax ID (matricule fiscal), and whether you invoice an association/school.
13. Can you provide references or photos of previous school, club or association orders?
14. Delivery to Tébourba (Manouba governorate): cost and options, or pickup location?
15. Who owns the artwork files and screens/digitised files, and can they be reused for a re-order?
16. Do you require written authorisation from the school for the use of its name or logo?
17. Quality and care: what are the wash instructions, and do you offer a replacement for defective pieces?
